Overview
Dumper Fuel Consumption Prediction Challenge
Mindshift Analytics × AI & SW Guild, IIT Madras

Fuel is one of the largest operating costs in open-cast iron ore mining. Large fleets of dumpers continuously transport material between excavation faces, stockpiles, crushers, and dump locations — consuming significant volumes of diesel daily. Even marginal improvements in fuel efficiency have direct impact on operating costs and sustainability.

Yet fuel consumption is hard to predict. The same dumper, on the same mine, can consume significantly different amounts of fuel depending on the route taken, the terrain it traverses, the operator behind the wheel, and operational conditions on any given shift.

The Challenge
Given telemetry, spatial map data, and refuelling records, your task is to estimate expected fuel consumption and identify abnormal excess fuel use at the dumper and route level.

Why It's Hard
Fuel consumption in dumpers is shaped by a complex interaction of factors:

Haul Road Design & Conditions — gradient and net lift between load and dump points, rolling resistance, road curvature, and maintenance quality all directly affect energy demand.

Mine Geometry — bench structure, elevation differences, terrain variability, and the distances between load and dump zones create route-specific difficulty profiles that no single metric captures.

Operator Behavior — acceleration and braking patterns, throttle discipline, idling time, and driving consistency introduce significant per-operator variation even on identical routes.

Operational Dynamics — loading efficiency, queuing delays, and cycle time variation add further noise to any consumption signal.

What You'll Build
A model (or set of models) that can accurately predict per-cycle fuel consumption from measurable signals — and generalize across dumpers, operators, and routes.

Description
Objective
Build models that estimate the fuel consumption of dumpers for haul cycles between loading and dumping locations, using telemetry, spatial data, and operational signals.

Specifically, your model should be able to:

Predict specific fuel consumed (in litres) for a dumper operating from a given load point to a dump point.
Predict generic fuel consumed (in litres) based on exposure (hours of operation), terrain (distance, lift, undulations, road zig-zag), and behavior (speed, stoppage density, etc.).
Validate against actuals by showing that aggregated model predictions align with daily or shift-wise actual fuel consumption.
Data Provided
Dumper Telemetry
High-frequency GPS and sensor data for each dumper, including timestamp, location, speed, altitude, heading, ignition status, and dump signal (where available). This is the primary data source for reconstructing haul cycles and deriving behavioral and terrain features.

Loader / Excavator Telemetry
Position and operating status data for loading equipment. Useful for identifying loading locations, inferring loading events, and understanding dumper-loader interaction.

Refuelling Transactions
Event-level records capturing the timestamp, dumper ID, and quantity of fuel added for each refuelling event. Used to anchor fuel balance and validate consumption estimates:

Actual Fuel Consumed = Opening Level + Refuelled − Closing Level
Dump Switch Analog Signal (available from March 1st onward, select dumpers)
An analog signal that activates during material discharge, providing explicit dump event markers. This is a key input for accurate cycle segmentation:

Each activation corresponds to a dump event
Haul cycles can be reconstructed as: Load → Travel → Dump
Patterns learned from this signal can be transferred to earlier data where the signal is unavailable
Spatial Data (GeoPackage)
Geospatial layers defining the mine structure, used for geofencing, route identification, and terrain context:

Layer	Description
haul_road	Haul road network
bench	Bench geometries
ob_dump	Overburden dump areas
stock / mineral_stock	Stockpile areas
ml_boundary	Mine boundary
cpu	Crushing plant
office	Infrastructure areas
All spatial layers are in UTM (EPSG:32645). Telemetry coordinates must be converted from WGS84 (EPSG:4326) before spatial analysis.

Key Predictive Features
The following parameters are established drivers of fuel consumption and should inform your feature engineering:

Dumper ID and Operator ID
Engine hours and idling hours
Lead distance and net lift
Gross elevation change
Standard deviation of altitude
Gradient / angle statistics
Speed statistics
Stop-go intensity
Load state (empty or loaded)

You are encouraged to derive additional features from the raw data, including:

Haul cycle segmentation (using dump switch and telemetry)
Distance travelled and net lift per cycle
Elevation variability, stop counts, idle duration
Speed statistics and route identifiers (cluster-to-cluster)
Loading and dumping dwell times
Terrain overlays, haul road snapping, geofence-aware features
